The **more convincing side is the opposition**: **Israel’s actions in Gaza may be devastating, unlawful in some respects, and potentially involve war crimes, but the arguments presented do not establish genocide on the evidence given.**

Here is why the opposition is stronger on the debate as framed:

### 1. The central legal issue is intent, and that was not proven
The pro side correctly explains that genocide requires the intent to destroy a protected group, in whole or in part. But the argument mostly relies on **inferences from destruction, civilian deaths, and rhetoric**. Those are serious indicators, but they are not enough by themselves to prove genocidal intent.

The opposition directly engages the key legal standard and makes the stronger point: a brutal military campaign can cause enormous civilian harm without meeting the specific threshold of genocide. That is the crucial distinction in this motion.

### 2. Civilian devastation does not automatically equal genocide
The pro side emphasizes the destruction of infrastructure, shortages of food and water, and mass casualties. Those facts are deeply troubling, but the opposition is right that **scale alone does not prove genocidal purpose**. In dense urban warfare, catastrophic civilian harm can occur even in wars aimed at defeating an enemy force.

The pro side’s argument would be stronger if it showed that destruction of civilians and civilian life was the goal rather than a consequence. Based on the arguments presented, that leap is not fully justified.

### 3. Rhetoric is relevant, but not decisive
The pro side points to dehumanizing or extreme statements by Israeli officials. That matters, but the opposition correctly notes that **individual inflammatory statements do not automatically establish state policy or genocidal intent**.

For genocide, the question is not whether some officials said appalling things; it is whether those statements reflect the actual purpose of the campaign. The opposition’s argument is stronger because it distinguishes between offensive rhetoric and legally relevant intent.

### 4. The opposition offers an alternative explanation consistent with the facts
The opposition gives a coherent alternative account: Israel says it is targeting Hamas, which embedded itself in civilian areas, creating severe civilian harm in the course of a military operation. Whether one finds that explanation convincing morally or strategically is separate, but as an argument against genocide, it is stronger than simply asserting that destruction implies intent.

A debater does not have to prove Israel’s conduct was admirable or proportionate to defeat the genocide claim. It is enough to show that the evidence presented does not conclusively establish the specific intent required.

### 5. The pro side overstates what international scrutiny proves
The pro side argues that international legal scrutiny shows the claim is substantial. That is true as far as it goes, but **being investigated is not proof**. The opposition properly points out that legal examination reflects seriousness, not a conclusion.
